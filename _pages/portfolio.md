My group combines global simulations, theory, and spacecraft observations to understand how plasma, magnetic fields, planetary rotation, atmospheres, and ionospheres interact across the Solar System. Our publications fall into five connected research themes.

      <p class="research-kicker">Giant planets and moons</p>
      <h2>Magnetospheres and aurorae</h2>
      <p>We investigate rapidly rotating magnetospheres as coupled systems in which internal plasma sources, solar-wind forcing, magnetic topology, reconnection, and plasma transport jointly control global dynamics and auroral morphology.</p>
      <h3>Core questions</h3>
      <ul class="research-focus-list">
        <li>How do rotation and internal mass loading reshape magnetopauses, cusps, and magnetotails?</li>
        <li>What links magnetic reconnection, current-sheet dynamics, and observable auroral structures?</li>
        <li>How do Jupiter, Saturn, and Ganymede reveal common and distinct plasma processes?</li>
      </ul>

        <li><strong>Unveiling the connection between the Alfvénic oval and the open–closed field line boundary at Ganymede.</strong> <em>The Astrophysical Journal</em> (2024).</li>

      <p class="research-kicker">Comparative planetary plasma</p>
      <h2>Solar-wind interaction with Venus and Mars</h2>
      <p>For planets without strong intrinsic magnetic fields, we model how the solar wind couples directly to ionospheres and induced magnetospheres. The work connects boundary instabilities and magnetotail reconnection to atmospheric escape and extreme space-weather responses.</p>
      <h3>Core questions</h3>
      <ul class="research-focus-list">
        <li>How do shocks, ICMEs, and solar-wind discontinuities reorganize induced magnetospheres?</li>
        <li>What roles do reconnection, Kelvin–Helmholtz instability, and turbulence play in plasma transport?</li>
        <li>How can multifluid global models quantify ion escape and planetary space-weather hazards?</li>
      </ul>
      <h3>Representative publications</h3>
      <ol class="research-publication-list">
        <li><strong>Shock-induced magnetic reconnection in the Venusian magnetotail.</strong> <em>Nature Communications</em> (2026).</li>
        <li><strong>Asymmetrically distributed Kelvin–Helmholtz instability at the Venusian magnetosphere.</strong> <em>Geophysical Research Letters</em> (2026).</li>
        <li><strong>Response of the Venusian plasma environment to the interplanetary coronal mass ejections on 5 November 2011.</strong> <em>Astronomy &amp; Astrophysics</em> (2026).</li>
        <li><strong>A new tool for understanding the solar wind–Venus interaction: Three-dimensional multifluid MHD model.</strong> <em>The Astrophysical Journal</em> (2023).</li>
        <li><strong>A simulation study of an HFA-like event at Mars.</strong> <em>The Astrophysical Journal</em> (2026).</li>

      <p class="research-kicker">Coupled geospace</p>
      <h2>Magnetosphere–ionosphere coupling</h2>
      <p>We study the two-way exchange of mass, momentum, and electromagnetic energy between magnetospheres and ionospheres. A central goal is to identify how ionospheric conductance and outflow feed back on reconnection, global convection, substorms, and auroral energy deposition.</p>
      <h3>Core questions</h3>
      <ul class="research-focus-list">
        <li>Where is Alfvénic power generated, transported, and deposited during disturbed conditions?</li>
        <li>How do ionospheric outflow and conductance regulate global magnetospheric dynamics?</li>
        <li>What physical processes create auroral streamers, asymmetries, and substorm-scale variability?</li>
      </ul>
      <h3>Representative publications</h3>
      <ol class="research-publication-list">
        <li><strong>Alfvénic Poynting flux characterized by a substorm-SMC cycle and ionospheric influence.</strong> <em>Space Weather</em> (2026).</li>

        <li><strong>What controls the dawn–dusk asymmetry of dayside Alfvénic power?</strong> <em>Geophysical Research Letters</em> (2024).</li>
        <li><strong>Ionospheric control of magnetotail reconnection.</strong> <em>Science</em> (2014).</li>
        <li><strong>Magnetotail origins of auroral Alfvénic power.</strong> <em>Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics</em> (2012).</li>

      <p class="research-kicker">Upper-atmosphere dynamics</p>
      <h2>Ionosphere–thermosphere coupling and space weather</h2>
      <p>Our coupled simulations trace how magnetospheric energy input, atmospheric waves, solar eclipses, and geomagnetic storms perturb the ionosphere and thermosphere. This work links fundamental coupling physics to satellite-drag and operational space-weather impacts.</p>
      <h3>Core questions</h3>
      <ul class="research-focus-list">
        <li>How do particle precipitation and traveling atmospheric disturbances alter thermospheric density?</li>
        <li>What controls high-latitude winds, ion-neutral coupling, and ionospheric irregularities?</li>
        <li>How can global models explain severe events affecting satellites and technological systems?</li>
      </ul>
      <h3>Representative publications</h3>

      <p class="research-kicker">Computational plasma physics</p>
      <h2>High-order numerical methods and global MHD</h2>
      <p>We develop conservative, geometry-aware numerical algorithms and scalable global models for plasma dynamics. GAMERA and GAMERA-OP are designed to preserve key physical structures while resolving multiscale dynamics on non-orthogonal and orthogonal curvilinear grids.</p>
      <h3>Core questions</h3>
      <ul class="research-focus-list">
        <li>How can finite-volume schemes remain accurate, stable, and conservative on complex planetary grids?</li>
        <li>How should numerical methods treat coordinate singularities, pressure anisotropy, and magnetic-field evolution?</li>
        <li>How can modern solvers connect fundamental algorithm development with mission-scale simulations?</li>
      </ul>
      <h3>Representative publications</h3>
      <ol class="research-publication-list">
        <li><strong>GAMERA-OP: A three-dimensional finite-volume magnetohydrodynamic solver for orthogonal curvilinear geometries.</strong> <em>The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series</em> (2026).</li>
        <li><strong>Enhanced partial donor cell method for hyperbolic equations in orthogonal curvilinear coordinates.</strong> <em>Computer Physics Communications</em> (2025).</li>
        <li><strong>Gas kinetic schemes for solving the magnetohydrodynamic equations with pressure anisotropy.</strong> <em>Journal of Computational Physics</em> (2023).</li>
        <li><strong>GAMERA: A three-dimensional finite-volume MHD solver for non-orthogonal curvilinear geometries.</strong> <em>The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series</em> (2019).</li>
        <li><strong>Conservative averaging-reconstruction techniques for three-dimensional finite-volume MHD solvers with axis singularity.</strong> <em>Journal of Computational Physics</em> (2019).</li>
